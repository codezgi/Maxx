PEEK vs Titanium Suture Anchors: Material Differences

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. PEEK vs Titanium Suture Anchors: Material Differences

Maxx Global Medical · 16 July 2026 · Last updated: 3 November 2026

PEEK and titanium are two common materials for suture anchors. This article outlines their material differences, imaging behaviour and the limits of the biomechanical literature.

In short: Titanium is a metal and may cause imaging artefacts; PEEK is a radiolucent polymer. Material strength and an anchor’s fixation strength in bone are not the same thing. This review did not find clinical outcome data directly comparing PEEK with titanium.

Titanium

ASTM F136 is the reference standard for the Ti-6Al-4V ELI alloy used in implant applications.[2] As a metal, it may cause artefacts on MRI and CT images.

PEEK

PEEK is a non-absorbable, radiolucent polymer. Containing no metal, it causes no metallic artefact; for the same reason the implant itself is not visible on plain radiographs. Its elastic modulus is closer to bone than that of metals.

Like any implant, PEEK has reported adverse events: the literature mentions osteolysis and cyst formation, and one case report describes a sterile tissue reaction.[3] A single case report does not establish frequency or causation.

What do the biomechanical data say?

In a network meta-analysis of rotator cuff anchors, PEEK anchors showed higher failure loads than biocomposite anchors. Metal anchors ranked first for least displacement, and stiffness did not differ significantly. PEEK anchors were more likely to pull out than biocomposite anchors.[1]

The study grouped anchors by material: size and shape differences within groups were not controlled, and the “metal” group was not separated specifically for titanium. Confidence in the evidence was generally very low.[1] These data therefore cannot answer “which material is better?”.

Some anchors combine both materials in one design, for example the Titanium Peek Knotless Pushlock. Material content is stated on the product page and label.

Who makes the choice?

Anchor selection depends on bone structure, joint, imaging needs and surgical technique, and rests with the healthcare professional. For the basics, see What Is a Suture Anchor?
